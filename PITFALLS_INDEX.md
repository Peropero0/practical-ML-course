# Catalog of pitfalls

Every exercise in the course ends with a **common pitfall**: the mistake that is really made and that usually gives no message. This is the complete list, by module, with a link to the solution where you see it live. Reread it before handing in any work.

## Module 1 · Linear regression

| ID | Exercise | Common pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | [OLS with the normal equation and with the SVD](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/A_from_scratch/A1_ols_normal_equation_and_svd.ipynb) | The conditioning problem does not concern only polynomials: it shows up whenever two or more features are nearly collinear or have very different scales. With the normal equation it happens silently: no error, just wrong coefficients. Scaling the features (and not using `np.linalg.inv`) reduces the risk but does not eliminate it. |
| A2 | [Gradient descent for linear regression](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/A_from_scratch/A2_gradient_descent.ipynb) | At the first `nan` in the loss you tend to blame the data or the code. With gradient descent the most frequent cause is a learning rate too large for the scale of the features. Before looking for bugs elsewhere, reduce lr by an order of magnitude and scale the features. |
| A3 | [Standard errors of the coefficients: closed formula versus bootstrap](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/A_from_scratch/A3_standard_errors_formula_and_bootstrap.ipynb) | The bootstrap must be done on the **rows** (x, y pairs). Resampling only the residuals is a different procedure (residual bootstrap) that presupposes homoscedasticity itself, the very assumption you want to test here. |
| B1 | [California housing: baseline, linear model and logarithmic target](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B1_california_baseline_and_log_target.ipynb) | Comparing two models with metrics computed in different units (one R² on the log, another on the prices) or choosing the target transformation by looking at the metric that the transformation itself optimizes. The right question is always: which error costs more in the real problem? |
| B2 | [Lasso and noise features: automatic selection and scale](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B2_lasso_and_noise_features.ipynb) | Letting `LassoCV` choose `alpha` does not fix a scale problem: it chooses the best alpha **for those features as they were**. The scaler goes in the pipeline (so it is fitted only on the training fold at every round of cross-validation), not applied by hand beforehand. |
| B3 | [Polynomial pipeline with Ridge and GridSearchCV](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B3_polynomial_ridge_gridsearch_pipeline.ipynb) | Reporting the RMSE of `search.best_score_` as the final estimate of the performance: it is the maximum among many combinations tried on the same folds, so it is optimistic. For the honest estimate you need the test set, touched only once (or a nested cross-validation). |
| C1 | [Collinearity: unstable coefficients, stable predictions](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/C_break_the_model/C1_collinearity_unstable_coefficients.ipynb) | Looking at the p-value or the sign of a single coefficient in the presence of high VIFs. A negative coefficient for a variable that should have a positive effect, together with a very positive coefficient for its "twin", is the typical symptom: it is not information about the world, it is an artifact of collinearity. |
| C2 | [A single point moves the line: leverage versus residual](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/C_break_the_model/C2_outliers_and_leverage.ipynb) | Looking for outliers by looking only at large residuals. A high-leverage point "pulls" the line toward itself and can have a modest residual: it is the worst case, because the residual plot looks fine. You need leverage and Cook's distance, not just the residuals. |
| C3 | [Too many features: when the training R² lies](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/C_break_the_model/C3_too_many_features_overfitting.ipynb) | Comparing models with a different number of features using the training R², or reporting the training R² as the "quality of the model" in a report. With p close to n it is a number that contains no information about generalization. |
| D1 | [Reading the coefficients: units, standardization and the logarithm](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/D_interpret/D1_reading_the_coefficients.ipynb) | Saying "the variable with the largest coefficient is the most important". And its twin: standardizing a binary variable and reading the result as the "effect of one standard deviation" makes little sense (for a 0/1 the standard deviation depends on the proportion of ones). For binary variables it is preferable to read directly the effect of the step from 0 to 1. |
| D2 | [Residual diagnostics: four mysteries](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/D_interpret/D2_residual_diagnostics_four_mysteries.ipynb) | Fixating on the Q-Q plot and on the normality of the residuals: in dataset A the normality test (Shapiro) rejects, in B it does not, but the problem of A is not the shape of the distribution, it is the non-constant variance. Normality is the least important of the assumptions for predicting. A test that fires does not tell you **which** assumption is violated: you need the right statistic. |
| D3 | [Anscombe: four datasets, one line, three wrong models](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/D_interpret/D3_anscombe_leverage_and_influence.ipynb) | Saying "I remove the outlier" without understanding whether it is a measurement error or a piece of reality. In III the point is probably an error (it must be corrected or investigated); in IV the isolated point **is the only information** we have about the relationship, and removing it means no longer having a model. |
| E1 | [The model that finds signal in noise](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E1_leakage_in_feature_selection.ipynb) | Thinking that "doing the cross-validation" is enough. Cross-validation only protects from evaluating on data seen in training: it does not protect from preprocessing and selection run beforehand. Rule of thumb: if a transformation has a `fit`, it must sit in a `Pipeline`. |
| E2 | [Regression metrics computed wrongly: four bugs](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E2_metrics_computed_wrongly.ipynb) | Computing the baseline with the mean of the test set. The improvement looks a bit smaller or larger than the true one and the difference is small: it is a bug that survives because it does not make anything collapse. The test set must not influence in any way any reference quantity. |
| E3 | [The scaler refitted on the test set](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E3_scaler_refitted_on_test.ipynb) | Standardizing "per fold" with `fit_transform` on every validation fold (it happens when you write the cross-validation by hand). The scaler must be fitted on the training part of the fold and applied to the validation: it is exactly what `cross_val_score` does with a Pipeline. |
| F1 | [OLS, Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet: which one wins, and when](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/F_compare/F1_ols_ridge_lasso_elasticnet_simulation.ipynb) | Concluding from a single repetition (or from a single real dataset) that "method X is the best". The differences between methods are often of the same order as the variability from one sample to another: without many repetitions and a large test set you see differences that do not exist. |
| F2 | [Ridge with and without scaling: the same penalty is not the same model](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/F_compare/F2_ridge_with_and_without_scaling.ipynb) | Scaling the features **before** the split or the cross-validation instead of inside the Pipeline (leakage: E3 and the lessons), or scaling the target without noticing. And the opposite: forgetting the scaler because "the features are all numeric already". The unit of measure always matters, for any model with a penalty on the weights. |
| F3 | [Mean versus median: OLS and quantile regression](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/F_compare/F3_mean_versus_median_ols_and_quantile_regression.ipynb) | Choosing the model by looking at the metric that the model optimizes. If the model minimizes the square of the errors, it almost certainly has the best RMSE: the comparison is rigged. The comparison metric must come from the cost of the error for the problem, and the model must be trained with the loss that reflects it. |
| R1 | [Warm-up: the first regression, from data to number](ML_course/module_01_linear_regression/solutions/B_with_sklearn/R1_warmup_the_first_regression.ipynb) | Reporting the training R² as "the quality of the model": it is always optimistic. And reading an R² without comparing it with the baseline. |

## Module 2 · Preprocessing: scaling, encoding categoricals, missing data

| ID | Exercise | Common pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | [Three scalers from scratch: fit, transform and the constant column case](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/A_from_scratch/A1_scalers_from_scratch.ipynb) | Computing the statistics on the complete dataset "for convenience" before the split. The model never sees the test set, but the scaler does: the statistic of the test set enters the training data. The effect on the metrics is small with scaling and large with encoders that use the target (exercise A3). |
| A2 | [OneHotEncoder from scratch: unknown categories and `drop`](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/A_from_scratch/A2_one_hot_encoder_from_scratch.ipynb) | Building the columns by looking at the test set too (for example `np.unique` on the union). With real data the test set often contains categories absent from the training set: the columns must come only from the training set, otherwise in production you will have a different number of columns from the one the model was trained with. |
| A3 | [Target encoding from scratch: smoothing and out-of-fold encoding](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/A_from_scratch/A3_target_encoding_from_scratch.ipynb) | Applying target encoding with `fit_transform` on the whole dataset before the split or the cross-validation. Even with smoothing, every row carries with it a part of its own target. It always goes inside the pipeline, with a transformer that uses the out-of-fold encoding during the fit (you will write it in exercise E3). |
| B1 | [Preprocessing a mixed dataset, one column type at a time](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B1_manual_preprocessing_mixed_dataset.ipynb) | Concatenating the blocks in a different order between training and test (for example `np.hstack([Z_nom_te, Z_num_te, ...])`). The model does not complain because the number of columns matches, but it applies every coefficient to the wrong column. |
| B2 | [Imputers compared: mean, median, KNN and iterative, with known truth](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B2_imputers_compared.ipynb) | Evaluating an imputer on the RMSE of all the cells (including the non-missing ones, which are unchanged) instead of only on the removed ones: the result looks better than it is. And forgetting that the error is computed against a truth that in real data you don't have: the simulation serves to choose the method, not to estimate the error in production. |
| B3 | [Skewed variables: log, Yeo-Johnson and quantiles, inside and outside the range](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B3_transformations_of_skewed_variables.ipynb) | Choosing the transformation by looking only at the skewness (`skew` close to zero) or only at the in-range test set. Neither tells you what happens when the production data leave the range: it is a common case for incomes, prices and counts. |
| C1 | [Features in euros sink the KNN: where the distance goes](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/C_break_the_model/C1_scaling_and_knn_distances.ipynb) | Scaling and considering the job done. Irrelevant features have the same weight as relevant ones in a Euclidean distance; in a real dataset with dozens of columns, most of them useless, the scaled KNN can still do badly. |
| C2 | [Encoding a nominal with integers: the invented order](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/C_break_the_model/C2_ordinal_on_nominal_variable.ipynb) | Using `LabelEncoder` or `OrdinalEncoder` "because it is more convenient" on a nominal and then passing the data to a linear model, a KNN or a network. No error, no warning: just a worse model, and nobody knows why. |
| C3 | [Imputation with the mean erases variance and correlation](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/C_break_the_model/C3_mean_imputation_distorts.ipynb) | Choosing the imputer by looking only at the error on the imputed values (RMSE, as in B2). An imputer with the lowest RMSE, like deterministic regression, produces a distribution with the wrong variability: the right criterion depends on the use you will make of the data. |
| D1 | [Reading a one-hot: names, memory and the dummy variable trap](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/D_interpret/D1_reading_a_one_hot_and_the_dummy_trap.ipynb) | Reading the coefficient of a dummy as "the effect of the category" without saying with respect to which reference category. If the baseline is chosen alphabetically (`fixed_term`) the choice is arbitrary: changing it changes all the coefficients, not the model. In reports always state the baseline. |
| D2 | [Diagnosing missingness mechanisms: what you can and cannot know](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/D_interpret/D2_missingness_mechanisms.ipynb) | Concluding "MCAR, so I can drop the rows" after running an underpowered test. And the opposite: correcting the p-values with Bonferroni and rejecting everything, when the real dependence is weak. In practice you combine the tests with domain knowledge and check **empirically** whether the imputation choice changes the performance. |
| E1 | [pd.get_dummies on training and test: same size, different columns](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E1_get_dummies_misaligned_columns.ipynb) | Calling `pd.get_dummies` "for convenience" during development and forgetting that in production the rows arrive one at a time (one region at a time, so a single column!). The code that works on a whole test set breaks silently, or obviously, on a single request. |
| E2 | [Coefficients with the wrong names: the column order after the ColumnTransformer](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E2_wrong_coefficient_names.ipynb) | Trusting the order of the columns "because it is the same as before anyway". With `remainder="passthrough"`, with transformers that change the number of columns (one-hot) or with blocks in a different order, that assumption is false. The rule: names are rebuilt from the transformer, never by hand. |
| E3 | [Target encoding before the cross-validation: a transformer inside the Pipeline](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E3_target_encoding_before_cv.ipynb) | Relying on the default `fit_transform` (`TransformerMixin` calls `fit(X, y).transform(X)`): in target encoding it means encoding the training set with the in-sample mapping, that is the very bug you were trying to avoid. For transformers that use `y` the `fit_transform` must be overridden. |
| F1 | [Which scaler for which model, with and without outliers](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/F_compare/F1_scaler_per_model_with_and_without_outliers.ipynb) | Comparing scalers on clean data and concluding that "they are the same", or that scaling is not needed. The differences show up with real data, which has outliers. When in doubt you measure in cross-validation, you don't choose out of habit. |
| F2 | [Six ways to encode `occupation` (40 levels), with two models](ML_course/module_02_preprocessing/solutions/F_compare/F2_encoders_for_high_cardinality.ipynb) | Choosing the encoder on the AUC of a single model, or on the value of a single fold. The differences between encoders are often of the order of 0.01: they must be read with the standard deviation across folds and repeated with different seeds before concluding. |

## Module 3 · Pipelines, cross-validation and hyperparameter search

| ID | Exercise | Common pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | [A Pipeline from scratch: who calls `fit` and who calls `transform`](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/A_from_scratch/A1_pipeline_from_scratch.ipynb) | Calling `fit` (instead of `fit_transform`) on the intermediate steps and then `transform` on the same data is equivalent. Calling `fit_transform` in **prediction** is not: it recomputes the statistics on the batch it receives, the bug of exercise E3 of module 2. |
| A2 | [K-fold, stratified K-fold and `cross_val_score` from scratch](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/A_from_scratch/A2_kfold_and_cross_val_score_from_scratch.ipynb) | Not shuffling the data (`shuffle=False`) when the rows are sorted by class, by time or by group: the folds become contiguous blocks with different distributions. It is the bug of exercise E3. |
| A3 | [GridSearchCV from scratch](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/A_from_scratch/A3_grid_search_from_scratch.ipynb) | Reporting `best_score_` as the final estimate. It is the maximum of many estimates on the same folds, so it is biased upward: you measure it in exercise C3. |
| B1 | [Titanic: a complete pipeline, three variants compared](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B1_titanic_complete_pipeline.ipynb) | Comparing variants by looking at the test set at every trial. The test set is used once; to choose among variants you use cross-validation on the training set. |
| B2 | [A single GridSearchCV to choose between different models](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B2_gridsearch_over_several_models.ipynb) | Forgetting `refit` with several metrics: if `scoring` is a dictionary, `refit` must say **which** metric to use to choose the `best_estimator_`. Without it, the search works but does not produce the final model. |
| B3 | [Custom features inside the pipeline, and saving the model for production](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B3_custom_features_and_saving.ipynb) | Transformation functions that modify the DataFrame in place. In a pipeline the input DataFrame could be the same object used elsewhere: `df.copy()` at the start avoids side effects that are hard to find. |
| C1 | [Temporal leakage: random K-fold on a time series](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/C_break_the_model/C1_temporal_leakage_kfold_on_series.ipynb) | Adding `t` (or the date) as a feature to a tree model and validating randomly: the tree learns to "remember" the periods already seen. In production `t` takes never-seen values and the model has nothing left to remember: a tree does not extrapolate. |
| C2 | [Group leakage: the same patient in training and in validation](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/C_break_the_model/C2_group_leakage_patients.ipynb) | Not having a `patient` column ("it is not needed as a feature") and so not thinking about grouping. The group must not be a feature: it is needed only by the splitter. If you don't pass it, the validation is silently optimistic. |
| C3 | [The optimism of best_score_: measuring it with a simulation](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/C_break_the_model/C3_best_score_optimism.ipynb) | Reporting `best_score_` in a report, or choosing between two tuning procedures by looking at their `best_score_`: you compare scores inflated in different ways. To compare procedures you need the nested estimate (or an untouched test set). |
| D1 | [Reading `cv_results_`: who really wins, and which parameter matters](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/D_interpret/D1_reading_cv_results.ipynb) | Taking `best_params_` as a discovery ("the optimal depth is 6"). With the variability of the folds the "optimal" depth changes if you change seed or sample: the correct message is "among these combinations there is no demonstrable difference". |
| D2 | [Validation and learning curves: diagnosing a model](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/D_interpret/D2_validation_and_learning_curves.ipynb) | Interpreting a learning curve with a single seed and no standard deviation. A gain of 0.005 is within the fold noise: decisions (collecting more data, changing model) must be made on differences that exceed the variability, and with repeated folds when needed. |
| E1 | [Optimizing and evaluating on the same data](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E1_search_and_evaluation_on_the_same_data.ipynb) | Redoing the search on all the data "after choosing" to produce the final model and then reporting the earlier test score. It is fine only if the reported score is the one obtained **before** retraining on everything: afterward, the test set is no longer independent. |
| E2 | [The ColumnTransformer that silently drops the most important columns](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E2_columntransformer_drops_columns.ipynb) | Thinking that "if a column is not needed, I don't list it": fine, but then dropping it must be a written decision (explicit `remainder="drop"`, maybe with a comment), not a side effect of a default. |
| E3 | [KFold on data sorted by class and `error_score=0` hiding everything](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E3_kfold_on_sorted_data_and_error_score.ipynb) | Sorting or grouping the data "for convenience" (by class, by customer, by date) before using `KFold` without shuffling. Cross-validation assumes the rows are interchangeable: if they are not, the splitter must be chosen deliberately (groups, time, stratification). |
| F1 | [Hold-out, 5-fold, 10-fold, repeated and leave-one-out: bias and variance of the estimate](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/F_compare/F1_validation_strategies_bias_and_variance.ipynb) | Choosing the validation scheme by the "magic number" (10-fold is the standard) without looking at the size of the dataset and the cost of the model. With small datasets it is better to repeat; with huge data a single hold-out is more than enough. |
| F2 | [GridSearchCV, RandomizedSearchCV and HalvingRandomSearchCV compared](ML_course/module_03_pipeline_and_validation/solutions/F_compare/F2_grid_random_halving_compared.ipynb) | Concluding that random search "is always better" from a single comparison. With many interacting hyperparameters, or a narrow and badly defined search space, a fine grid can make the difference. The point is knowing how much budget you need, not believing a rule. |

## Module 4 · Logistic regression and classification metrics

| ID | Exercise | Common pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | [Logistic regression with gradient descent](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/A_from_scratch/A1_logistic_with_gradient_descent.ipynb) | Implementing the logistic gradient with the wrong sign or without dividing by `n`: the loss jumps or does not go down, and you can't tell whether it is the step's fault or the formula's. The check with finite differences should always be the **first** test. |
| A2 | [Confusion matrix, ROC curve and average precision from scratch](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/A_from_scratch/A2_metrics_and_roc_curve_from_scratch.ipynb) | Computing the ROC by sorting the scores and taking a point for every **example** instead of every **distinct value**: with rounded or discrete scores (trees!) the curve comes out different from scikit-learn's and the AUC slightly wrong, with no error. |
| B1 | [A complete logistic on the loans: pipeline and ablation study](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B1_loans_pipeline_and_ablation.ipynb) | Filling the missing values with the median **without indicators** and concluding that "income matters little": with the imputation you replaced the signal (whoever does not declare) with a typical value equal for everyone. The AUC without indicators is noticeably lower, and no error tells you so. |
| B2 | [Choosing the threshold with the real costs](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B2_optimal_threshold_with_costs.ipynb) | Choosing the threshold by looking at the ROC curve "at the point closest to the top left corner": it is the threshold that assumes equal costs and 50% prevalence. With real costs and prevalences the right point of the curve is another one. |
| B3 | [Multiclass classification: handwritten digits](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/B_with_sklearn/B3_multiclass_digits_and_choice_of_c.ipynb) | Reporting only the accuracy on a multiclass problem: a class with recall 0.6 can vanish in the average if it is rare. For every multiclass problem always look at recall and precision **per class** before trusting a single number. |
| C1 | [Perfect separation: when the logistic becomes very sure and wrong](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/C_break_the_model/C1_perfect_separation_and_extreme_probabilities.ipynb) | Raising `C` "to make the model converge better" or removing the regularization to "have pure coefficients". With small data or separable classes it produces enormous, meaningless coefficients, and the solver stops because of the iteration limit, not because it found the minimum. |
| C2 | [The same model at different prevalences: the AUC stays, the precision collapses](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/C_break_the_model/C2_prevalence_and_precision.ipynb) | Evaluating a rare-event model (fraud, failures, diseases) on a balanced or rebalanced test set and reporting precision and accuracy as if they held in production. AUC and recall are preserved, precision and accuracy are not: they must be computed on the **real prevalence**. |
| D1 | [Reading the coefficients: odds ratios, collinearity and omitted variables](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/D_interpret/D1_coefficients_odds_ratios_and_omitted_variables.ipynb) | Reporting "the effect of age on default" by reading a coefficient of a model that also contains a highly correlated variable. A table of coefficients is a description **of the model**, not of reality, and it is not causal even if the model is well estimated. |
| D2 | [Calibration: who tells the truth when they say 30%?](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/D_interpret/D2_calibration_of_five_models.ipynb) | Choosing the model with the best AUC and using its probabilities as risk estimates. The AUC knows nothing about the scale of the probabilities: a model with AUC 0.92 can say "90%" for cases that are positive 60 times out of 100. |
| D3 | [Reading a 10x10 confusion matrix with two rare classes](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/D_interpret/D3_multiclass_metrics_with_rare_classes.ipynb) | Reporting the accuracy of a multiclass problem with imbalanced classes: the rare classes can have recall 0.5 and not move the total. Always report the per-class table. |
| E1 | [The income in euros that wipes out the amount/income ratio](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E1_regularization_without_scaling.ipynb) | Regularizing (`Ridge`, `Lasso`, `LogisticRegression`, `SVC`, ...) without standardizing, "because the features are all positive anyway" or "because the model converges all the same". Convergence has nothing to do with it: it is the penalty that depends on the scale. |
| E2 | [`predict_proba[:, 1]` is not always the probability you think](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E2_predict_proba_columns_and_classes.ipynb) | Writing `predict_proba(X)[:, 1]` as if it were an always-valid idiom. It is valid with a 0/1 target and `classes_ = [0, 1]`; with booleans, text or classes not ordered the way you expect, it is not. Check `classes_` at least once per project. |
| E3 | [100% recall with a random forest: the rebalancing done before the split](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/E_find_the_bug/E3_oversampling_before_the_split.ipynb) | Balancing the dataset "to prepare it" in a data-cleaning notebook and then doing the split in the modeling notebook. The balanced dataset must never exist outside a training pipeline. |
| F1 | [No penalty, L2, L1 and elastic net with few true features and many useless ones](ML_course/module_04_logistic_regression/solutions/F_compare/F1_none_l2_l1_elasticnet.ipynb) | Concluding from a single dataset that "L1 is better than L2" (or vice versa). The answer depends on how the signal is made (sparse or diffuse) and on how many examples you have: here the difference is large because six features out of a hundred really matter.
